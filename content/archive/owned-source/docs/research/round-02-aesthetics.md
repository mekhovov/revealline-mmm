# Round 02 — Fifteen worlds for the same territory game

Research date: 12 September 2026. This is a design exploration, not an implementation specification. The first twelve names match the original concept boards for this round. Names, sound directions, mechanics, and reward ideas below are proposals. Reference games are a curated set of useful precedents, not a ranking of all popular pixel-art games.

## Recommended direction

Keep all three initial families: Ukrainian FPV action, Ukrainian living folk art, and a Coupa savings adventure. Develop an additional playful retro anthology around them. The strongest common identity is **a beautiful world gradually brought to life by the lines you draw**. This ties precision, danger, curiosity, and collection together without making every theme another recolored military board.

Treat each world as a small album: a distinct avatar, line material, capture animation, enemy vocabulary, musical arrangement, scene collection, and one optional rule twist. Every world can support classic rules. Its optional twist should be explicitly selected and separately scored, so a player can enjoy any artwork without relearning the game.

The next visual comparison should prioritize 02 KYIV AFTER HOURS, 05 PETRYKIVKA BLOOM, 08 MIDNIGHT CIRCUIT, and 12 COUPA CITY. Together they test dark city atmosphere, decorative illustration, crisp arcade abstraction, and approachable corporate character art. 06 PYSANKA COSMOS offers the most distinctive long-term signature: drawing a safe boundary is conceptually close to wax-resist image revelation.

## Ukrainian worlds

### 01 — DAYBREAK

- **Look:** broad ochre fields, ink-blue rivers, warm clouds, tiny white houses; restrained embroidered framing. A luminous white trail remains visible on both yellow and blue. Landscape details are denser in revealed regions and quieter under the playfield.
- **Player / threats:** a small, expressive Ukrainian FPV drone against distinct invading patrol drones, angular sweepers, and clearly telegraphed interference zones. Readable silhouettes carry enemy identity.
- **Reveal / reward:** an obscured landscape becomes a breathing scene with wind, water, and people returning to ordinary life. Completing a regional set creates a panoramic postcard.
- **Sound:** original tracker drums, bandura-like plucks, and a rising morning melody; the completed enclosure resolves a suspended chord.
- **Distinctive rule:** optional relay objectives reward a meaningful enclosure, alongside captured area. The basic mode retains ordinary Xonix capture.
- **Best showcase:** a TV or desktop panorama; a portrait phone uses one composed scene rather than a crop of the panorama.

### 02 — KYIV AFTER HOURS

- **Look:** wet paving, cyan shop windows, amber apartment lights, violet skies, chestnut silhouettes, tram-wire geometry. The city feels inhabited rather than an anonymous neon metropolis.
- **Player / threats:** a courier-like FPV drone; hostile searchlight sweeps, interference sprites, and border patrols with different silhouettes.
- **Reveal / reward:** each enclosure switches on a block: a bakery window, tram stop, courtyard cinema, roof garden. Completing a district changes the background from midnight toward dawn.
- **Sound:** late-night synth-pop and muted breakbeats; tram-bell-like tones become a completion motif. Original compositions throughout.
- **Distinctive rule:** an optional district-power chain rewards enclosing neighboring relay nodes in a sequence. The sequence is visible before the attempt.
- **Best showcase:** desktop or handheld landscape; tall phone art becomes a single facade with stacked illuminated windows.
- **References:** the human-scale towns and characterfulness of [Eastward](https://eastwardgame.com/); neo-noir tension and decisive action from [Katana ZERO](https://www.devolverdigital.com/games/katana-zero). These are design translations, not claims that those games use capture mechanics.

### 03 — CARPATHIAN SIGNAL

- **Look:** layered pine and beech silhouettes, pale morning mist, red woven details, wooden shelters, mountain ridges. A cream-and-vermilion drone stands apart from green scenery.
- **Player / threats:** an FPV messenger drone; abstract storm wisps, gust fronts, and mechanical pursuers. Friendly birds are scenery or collectibles, visually separate from threats.
- **Reveal / reward:** fog lifts from a valley; clearings gain tiny sheep, cabins, and moving streams. Collected scenes assemble into a journey along a mountain ridge.
- **Sound:** airy flute-like phrases, plucked strings, warm bass, and restrained percussion; a distant horn-like call marks a new region.
- **Distinctive rule:** optional wind windows visibly change a lane's traversal cost, encouraging the player to choose the moment to leave safety. No hidden drift in the classic controls.
- **Best showcase:** tablet portrait and desktop landscape both suit layered mountain compositions.
- **Grounding:** the Carpathian forest reference is natural geography, while the fantasy hazards are inventions. UNESCO documents the [Carpathian and European primeval beech forest heritage](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1133). [Owlboy](https://www.owlboygame.com/) is a useful precedent for a flying protagonist and rich pixel landscapes.

### 04 — BLACK SEA

- **Look:** ultramarine water, peach facades, chalk-white gulls, coral buoys, dark harbor silhouettes, gold sunlight reflected in stepped pixel clusters. Water is animated selectively.
- **Player / threats:** a coastal reconnaissance drone; hostile patrol sprites, drifting hazards, and readable current bands.
- **Reveal / reward:** each enclosure restores a section of harbor, lighthouse, or seaside postcard. One completed scene could become an animated souvenir with boats returning through a cleared channel.
- **Sound:** warm electric piano, mellow synth bass, sea percussion, and a clear lighthouse-like completion tone.
- **Distinctive rule:** optional lighthouse objectives reveal distant board hazards before the next cut; navigation becomes a sequence of deliberate safe openings.
- **Best showcase:** widescreen tablet/desktop, with a separately composed lighthouse card for portrait phones.
- **Grounding:** UNESCO describes Odesa as a Black Sea port with an eclectic, multicultural architectural fabric and tree-lined street grid. These are more specific prompts than generic tropical palms. [Historic Centre of Odesa](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1703/).

### 05 — PETRYKIVKA BLOOM

- **Look:** large curling leaves, fantastic birds, berry clusters, and spectacular flowers on cream or deep charcoal. Use ornamental clusters as the revealed picture; keep moving hazards outside dense decorative texture.
- **Player / threats:** a tiny pollinator-like FPV drone; ink blots, mechanical beetles, and dark creeping tendrils.
- **Reveal / reward:** the line turns into a stem; the claimed region blooms in a controlled wave. Completed works form a decorative garden collection, with a quiet gallery mode for close inspection.
- **Sound:** bright plucks, woodblock-like percussion, birdlike synth ornaments, and an additional harmony for each successful chain.
- **Distinctive rule:** optional flower-family objectives reward enclosing complementary bloom nodes. Do not require recognizing subtle colors: each family also has a different silhouette.
- **Best showcase:** portrait phones and tablets; particularly strong as menu and album art.
- **Grounding:** Petrykivka is associated with fantastic flowers and natural motifs. Its decorative composition is distinct from geometric embroidery. [UNESCO Petrykivka entry](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/petrykivka-decorative-painting-as-a-phenomenon-of-the-ukrainian-ornamental-folk-art-00893).

### 06 — PYSANKA COSMOS

- **Look:** black lacquer, cream lines, vermilion diamonds, gold suns, small celestial motifs, and a decorated drone. The game board stays geometrically clear while the gallery presents the completed artwork on a turning egg-shaped object.
- **Player / threats:** an orbital FPV drone carrying a wax-like light; ink comets, crawling fractures, and eclipse sweepers.
- **Reveal / reward:** boundaries protect layers of an image; completing a stage peels back a color layer. A final ceremonial reveal transforms the level's pattern into a collectible pysanka.
- **Sound:** bell-like FM synthesis, circular arpeggios, soft hand percussion, and a deep resonant completion chord.
- **Distinctive rule:** an optional multi-layer mode reuses the familiar enclosure action over two or three revealed layers. Preserve a flat logical board; a spherical shader must not distort threat locations.
- **Best showcase:** portrait collection cards; landscape retains a readable rectangular arena with the completed egg in a side panel.
- **Grounding:** the repeated use of wax and dye is documented tradition; converting it to a layered arcade mechanic is our proposal. [UNESCO Pysanka entry](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/pysanka-ukrainian-tradition-and-art-of-decorating-eggs-02134).

## Playful worlds beyond the initial Ukrainian collection

### 07 — AFTERSCHOOL ARCADE

- **Look:** warm wood desks, translucent plastic, sticker sheets, CRT cabinets, cassette cases, school-holiday sunlight. Favor the memory of playing in the 1990s over a wall of glowing gridlines.
- **Player / threats:** a tiny toy plane or arcade cursor; wind-up robots, pixel bugs, and paper monsters.
- **Reveal / reward:** uncover miniature scenes of a perfect summer afternoon. Each finished album adds a cartridge to an imaginary shelf, with a tiny original box illustration.
- **Sound:** crunchy FM bass, compact drum-machine loops, cheerful end-of-level jingles, and occasional cassette-button clicks in menus.
- **Distinctive rule:** short optional challenge cartridges impose one clearly explained modifier. Players can choose a favorite cartridge immediately and pursue medals later.
- **Best showcase:** handheld landscape; portrait uses a board above a tactile-looking control panel.
- **Reference:** [UFO 50](https://50games.fun/) deliberately combines an 8-bit visual vocabulary with modern ideas and a fictional 1980s game-company history. Adapt the sense of discovering a library; do not require a fifty-game production scope.

### 08 — MIDNIGHT CIRCUIT

- **Look:** near-black arena, ice-white player, cyan safe lines, magenta danger, amber score flashes. Surround the arena with a restrained synthwave city or a computer motherboard.
- **Player / threats:** a tiny light-cycle-like cursor; geometric sentries, plasma knots, and perimeter sparks.
- **Reveal / reward:** circuitry wakes beneath captured cells, exposing chrome portraits, city illustrations, or a glowing digital landscape. Replay snapshots become attractive share cards.
- **Sound:** dark synthwave, gated drums, short bass hits on close calls, and an especially satisfying circuit-lock sound.
- **Distinctive rule:** optional circuit chains connect enclosed nodes for score multipliers. The trail is always the brightest continuous mark.
- **Best showcase:** strongest small-screen readability; excellent for TV, Steam Deck, and keyboard play.
- **References:** [Narita Boy](https://www.team17.com/games/narita-boy) explicitly builds around an 80s digital-world fantasy, hand-drawn pixel animation, and synth music. [Cyber Shadow](https://www.yachtclubgames.com/games/cyber-shadow/) supplies a contrasting, tighter action vocabulary.

### 09 — ABYSSAL GARDEN

- **Look:** indigo depth, luminous coral, blue-green water, peach anemones, and tiny golden eyes. A small submarine is cream-yellow so it cannot disappear into the scenery.
- **Player / threats:** a friendly research submersible; electric jellyfish, pressure waves, and drifting mechanical debris.
- **Reveal / reward:** an apparently empty patch of sea becomes a living ecosystem. Completed areas add a creature to an illustrated field guide and a habitat to a personal aquarium.
- **Sound:** soft sonar, glassy synths, muffled percussion, and a warm surface melody between runs.
- **Distinctive rule:** optional air-pocket objectives encourage staged routes. Keep oxygen pressure as an optional expedition rule rather than a penalty in relaxing classic play.
- **Best showcase:** portrait phone as a depth journey; tablet as a richly animated reef window.
- **Reference:** [DAVE THE DIVER](https://www.mintrocketgames.com/en/) demonstrates a rewarding alternation between underwater activity and a characterful hub. It uses a hybrid visual presentation; our proposal can remain a lightweight 2D game.

### 10 — ORBITAL POST

- **Look:** cream spacecraft, orange mail labels, midnight space, lavender moons, chunky stars, and illustrated cosmic stamps. The fantasy is cheerful postal science fiction.
- **Player / threats:** a tiny mail shuttle; orbiting debris, space eels, and malfunctioning sorting robots.
- **Reveal / reward:** enclose delivery beacons to unveil alien postcards. Each world adds an illustrated stamp, a short message, and one decorative part for the ship's cabin.
- **Sound:** buoyant FM arpeggios, radio chirps, soft brass-like synths, and a postal-stamp thump on completion.
- **Distinctive rule:** optional delivery manifests ask the player to enclose two or three marked addresses together. Different arrangements create route decisions without extra action buttons.
- **Best showcase:** desktop wide-screen for a star chart beside the board; portrait phone for stamp-book progression.
- **References:** [Starbound](https://playstarbound.com/) supports the appeal of varied planetary biomes and collectable discoveries; [Star Renegades](https://rawfury.com/games/star-renegades/) is a reference for the expressive science-fiction end of modern pixel art, not a reason to adopt turn-based combat.

### 11 — MOSS AND MAGIC

- **Look:** oversized ferns, mushrooms, amber lanterns, dusty rose flowers, beetle-shell blues, and mossy ruins. Frame the board as a tiny clearing rather than an enormous open world.
- **Player / threats:** a lantern-carrying firefly or small woodland familiar; sleepy slimes, rolling acorns, and wandering thorn wisps.
- **Reveal / reward:** claimed soil grows a miniature garden. Completing a habitat attracts a new harmless creature and contributes to a personal terrarium.
- **Sound:** cozy woodwind-like synth leads, plucked patterns, and a soft rhythm section. Big captures sound like a musical bloom.
- **Distinctive rule:** optional seed pairs produce a special plant when enclosed together. Choose one pairing rule per board to keep it understandable.
- **Best showcase:** phone portrait and tablet; good candidate for a calm, untimed mode alongside normal arcade difficulty.
- **Reference:** [Owlboy](https://www.owlboygame.com/) shows that a flying avatar, expressive companions, and detailed pixel scenery can share a coherent world. Use that characterful craft; create our own creatures.

### 12 — COUPA CITY

- **Look:** approachable blue-and-white city, warm shop interiors, clear gold value markers, friendly people, and an optimistic miniature skyline. Avoid turning the playfield into a dense corporate dashboard.
- **Player / threats:** a Coupa-inspired mascot, an approved Navi asset, or a clearly provisional custom savings drone; invoice gremlins, duplication sprites, subscription leaks, and runaway cost bubbles.
- **Reveal / reward:** each enclosed opportunity funds a visible improvement: a client cafe reopens, a factory adds cleaner equipment, a clinic gets a bright new room. Fictional amounts express game score, not claims about customer savings.
- **Sound:** playful jazz-funk synthesis, marimba-like phrases, cash-register percussion used sparingly, and a growing town theme.
- **Distinctive rule:** optional contracts reward enclosing matching opportunities, while risk pockets tempt the player into a larger cut. Client stories make saving value meaningful beyond an increasing number.
- **Best showcase:** browser on an office laptop; phone for a short three-board challenge; TV for an event tournament.
- **Reference:** [Eastward](https://eastwardgame.com/) is useful for lively urban vignettes and warm characters. The corporate rules, mascot interpretation, and client scenes here are original proposals.

## Three additional directions to visualize next

### 13 — ÖRNEK GARDENS

**Crimean Tatar identity:** keep the title and cultural authorship explicit. Cream, rose, turquoise, leaf green, warm metalwork, and carefully composed floral ornament create a more delicate visual rhythm than Petrykivka. A copper bird-like drone traces a bright line; ink storms and thorn tangles obstruct it. Completed cuts reveal garden panels and intimate courtyard scenes. A proposed optional objective pairs marked flower nodes to complete a composition; the album can explain the artist's chosen symbolic relationships. Sound could combine original plucked-string phrases with gentle electronic percussion, developed with a Crimean Tatar musician. Strong on portrait phone and tablet.

**Grounding:** Örnek is a Crimean Tatar system of meaningful symbols, composed into narratives and used across multiple crafts. UNESCO distinguishes geometric ornaments associated with weaving from floral ornaments used in other crafts. Do not label it a generic Ukrainian flower pattern, or invent traditional meanings for arbitrary reward icons. [UNESCO Örnek entry](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/ornek-a-crimean-tatar-ornament-and-knowledge-about-it-01601?RL=01601).

### 14 — KOSIV KILN

**Hutsul ceramic craft:** warm ivory, moss green, yellow ochre, scratch-drawn brown outlines, birds, animals, people, and tiles. A tiny ceramic-looking drone or mechanical bird leaves a dark etched line. Glaze spills and ash sprites threaten the open cut. Enclosures fire into glazed tiles and assemble an animated tiled stove or illustrated village panel. Optional kiln pulses briefly energize clearly marked lanes; timing changes score, while normal movement remains precise. Ceramic taps, woody percussion, and rounded synth bass give it a tactile sound. Particularly distinctive on an iPad gallery and on a small phone where thick outlines help.

**Grounding:** Kosiv ceramics use a creamy white clay layer, scratched contours, characteristic green and yellow coloring, and figurative narratives connected to Hutsul life and the surrounding world. The arcade kiln mechanic is invented. [UNESCO Kosiv ceramics](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/tradition-of-kosiv-painted-ceramics-01456?RL=01456).

### 15 — DNIPRO MOSAIC

**Optimistic Ukrainian science fiction:** large tessellated shapes, petrol blue, burnt orange, chalk, oxidized copper, river forms, workshops, observatories, and imagined solar infrastructure. The player is a small maintenance drone; arcs, broken circuits, and industrial automata obstruct repairs. Captured sections restore a monumental mosaic whose figures and machines begin moving. Optional energy networks connect enclosed nodes and visibly change the finished city's lighting. Sound combines mechanical rhythm, warm analog synthesis, and spacious chords. Excellent desktop/TV composition and a strong foil to the fine detail of floral packs.

**Grounding:** this is an original retrofuturist world. The Ukrainian Institute documents Mykhaylo Boychuk's synthesis of monumental art and Ukrainian folk sources, including work that extended into everyday living space. That supports researching a distinct Ukrainian monumental vocabulary; it does not establish our invented city, technology, or palette as historical fact. [Ukrainian Institute — Mykhaylo Boychuk](https://uaview.ui.org.ua/artist/Boichuk-Mykhaylo).

## Cross-device visual rules worth deciding before building

These are design recommendations for the next visual tests, not measured device-performance claims:

1. **One danger grammar across all worlds.** Bright continuous player trail, clearly different enemy silhouettes, patterned dangerous regions, and quieter revealed artwork. Use symbols and motion as well as color.
2. **Separate illustration detail from gameplay resolution.** A richly drawn reveal can sit under a coarse, readable logical board. Test the smallest intended phone before adding more decoration.
3. **Compose for three canvases.** A portrait cover, a landscape postcard, and a square crop should be authored or approved for every major scene. Do not let a phone automatically crop the only important subject away.
4. **Keep typography crisp.** Pixel display type for short headings; a readable companion face for settings, accessibility, and longer descriptions. Regional motifs should frame controls, not become controls.
5. **Modern atmosphere can be inexpensive.** Use pre-rendered pixel lighting, small animated regions, palette animation, and a few foreground particles. Effects such as glare, chromatic offset, CRT scanlines, and shake should be adjustable.
6. **Audio must reinforce rules.** Every theme needs equivalent warning, open-trail, capture, life-loss, and completion cues, even when their instruments differ. Preserve clarity at phone-speaker volumes.
7. **A still image is only the first gate.** Review one animated capture, one crowded danger moment, a paused screen, a tiny-screen screenshot, and the final postcard before declaring a style production-ready.

## Suggested next decisions

- Choose two Ukrainian worlds and two broader worlds for the next close-up comparison.
- Choose whether the default reward is a finished illustration, an animated living scene, or a small collectible object; the recommendation is an animated scene plus a compact collectible card.
- Choose the strongest emotional range: hopeful restoration, pure arcade exhilaration, cozy discovery, or a selectable mixture. Each can sit above the same capture rules.
- Compare the same exact board state in the selected styles. Keep player position, trail, enemies, percentage revealed, and UI content fixed so the comparison tests art rather than unrelated layouts.
