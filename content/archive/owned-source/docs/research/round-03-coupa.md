# Round 03 — Coupa: restore a living business network

Research date: 12 September 2026. Status: concept research and proposals, not an approved Coupa product, brand treatment, or implementation. This develops the Coupa BSM + AI family alongside the user's three other selected families: FPV, Ukrainian culture, and 1980s/1990s nostalgia.

## Recommended identity

**Working title: COUPA — FLOW CITY.** A pixel-art business world has become fragmented by bottlenecks, duplicate data, and broken routes. The player closes territory cuts to restore useful connections. Procurement districts open, suppliers become reachable, matched documents align, and a sleeping city starts moving again.

The satisfying reward is visible activity: trains depart, factories light up, shop shutters open, deliveries arrive, and people use the things they requested. Money can be represented by fictional value points, but a coin is not the only reward or the whole premise. This gives the theme human purpose and turns Coupa's connected-process idea into a visual playground.

**Core fantasy:** “Make the whole network work beautifully.”

The following gameplay is invented. It does not simulate production Coupa behavior, perform real transactions, estimate customer savings, or claim that a game score corresponds to business value. Real products supply concepts and terminology; the arcade rules remain their own system.

The business layer should stay understandable without domain knowledge. On the live board use short verbs such as **Connect**, **Match**, **Restore**, and **Deliver**. Optional collection notes can explain how the real concept inspired the level.

## Four enemy roles

Use business problems as fictional hazards. Customers, suppliers, employees, and approvers are beneficiaries and participants in the recovered world, not enemies.

| Enemy | Readable silhouette and motion | Arcade behavior | Business metaphor and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Duplicate Pair** | Two magenta paper kites with offset rectangular outlines. | Roam together, separate at a clearly telegraphed interval, then rejoin. The pairing creates a temporary opening for a cut. | Duplicated records. This does not assert a particular Coupa duplicate-detection algorithm. |
| **Bottleneck Crawler** | Amber hourglass-shaped tram moving on safe boundary rails. | Follows the perimeter and periodically pauses before changing direction. | Delay and waiting. It follows the boundary-threat role; it is not an illustration of a person blocking approval. |
| **Exception Emitter** | Stationary red-violet document press with a large warning chevron. | Emits slow, readable pulses along marked lanes. Validly capturing its cells disables it. | An unresolved exception. It is a capturable device, never a hidden territory-fill blocker. |
| **Disruption Cloud** | A compact jagged slate cloud with a bright center and a broad movement warning. | Sweeps a predictable corridor across the open field; later levels combine its timing with other threats. | An abstract interruption to a supply route. It makes no prediction about real suppliers or risk. |

Moving enemies designated as fill blockers must share a clear mark across every theme. The stationary emitter must have a distinct shape and role. Its deactivation happens **after** the capture rule has determined the new safe cells.

## Four module choices

The player picks at most one module between boards. These are arcade abilities with AI-inspired names, not announced or emulated Coupa features. A deterministic game preview can represent “insight”; a live AI service is not needed to make the mechanic work.

| Module | Concrete gameplay effect | Decision it encourages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Forecast Lens** | Briefly displays the next short segment of one compatible enemy's deterministic path. | Commit to a route based on a clearer timing window. Do not show fake certainty for randomized motion. |
| **Match Link** | Closing a region containing a marked pair of related document tokens grants one ability recharge. | Plan a larger cut that includes useful objects, instead of taking only the nearest strip. |
| **Flow Relay** | Once a relay is captured, speed rises on that recovered safe route for a limited time. | Reposition to a better departure point. The boost does not alter live-trail speed or fill geometry. |
| **Scenario Preview** | At a route-choice screen, reveals the next board's objective and one otherwise hidden hazard placement before the player picks a branch. | Choose an expedition path that fits the current build. It does not solve the board or optimize a real supply chain. |

Navi-inspired guidance belongs at the safe moments: “These two stations match,” “This route is shorter but crosses a moving hazard,” or “Restore this relay to reopen the lane.” Keep these lines brief and optional. They are authored explanations of game rules, not responses from the real Coupa product.

## Artwork, collections, and repeat-play rewards

**Primary collection: The City That Works.** Each completed board produces a detailed pixel-art postcard; finishing a chapter combines several into a larger animated panorama. A returning player sees consequences: the same district has become active, with people, transport, lights, music, and small visual jokes.

- **Procurement postcards:** creative studio, bicycle workshop, busy café, community lab, festival backstage.
- **Sourcing postcards:** island market, night harbor, maker district, textile studio, a calm logistics interchange.
- **Invoicing/payment postcards:** paper observatory, clockwork archive, illuminated bridge, fountain plaza.
- **Supply-chain postcards:** mountain railway, river port, greenhouse delivery route, coastal warehouse town.
- **Optional mastery rewards:** a route stamp, an animated scene detail, an alternate original music arrangement, a title-screen scene, or a completed network map.

The reward for better play can be a more intricate optional animation or a medal. Basic completion should already reveal a satisfying full picture. Artwork already earned remains in the collection after an expedition failure.

Scoring can use **Flow**, **Connections**, **Precision**, or fictional in-game savings amounts, including currency-themed presentation if desired. The game's score represents its own economy, not measured savings for real customers. The user explicitly wants saving clients money to remain part of the fantasy.

## Three aesthetic treatments within the Coupa family

| Treatment | Visual mood | Best use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Flow City** — recommended | Rich blue night sky, warm window lights, mint routes, cobalt buildings, restrained coral threats; 16-bit city detail with clean arcade geometry. | Main Coupa campaign, desktop/handheld play, broad appeal. |
| **Paper Circuit** | Ivory paper layers, ink-blue outlines, cyan fold lines, colorful tabs, miniature stamps and origami machines. | Invoicing/Matchworks collections; strong contrast and charming details on phones. |
| **Navi Observatory** — working concept label | Deep indigo, luminous analytical constellations, modular floating districts, silver/cyan trails, a few magenta hazards. | AI/scenario expedition mode and a more futuristic visual variation. |

These palettes are creative proposals, not verified official brand token values. The main board must retain the same contrast hierarchy across them: player and live trail first, threats second, objectives third, revealed decorative artwork beneath.

Music can combine original FM-synth bass, soft tracker percussion, warm electric-piano chords, and short bright arpeggios. Capturing connected districts adds synchronized instruments until the completed city has a full groove. Avoid cash-register sounds as the dominant reward; machinery starting, a tram bell, paper folding, and a resolved chord give more varied feedback.

## Suggested next concept storyboard

Show **one Matchworks level** across four frames:

1. An unfinished paper city under dark interference; three clearly marked stations and one exception emitter.
2. The player draws a bold right-angle cut while the Duplicate Pair separates.
3. Closure captures the intended region; the emitter switches off, matched stations light, and a route becomes safe.
4. The completed picture shows a lively neighborhood and a short results card: recovered area, route stamp, and optional next module.

Use the same board on a portrait phone and landscape screen. Only the presentation changes. The exact fill policy must be agreed separately and shown correctly; none of the business metaphors is allowed to decide the capture outcome secretly.
