# Round 03 — four theme families, Ukrainian FPV first

Date: 12 September 2026. Status: visual brainstorming, not an approved implementation specification.

## Confirmed steering

The user selected all three motivations: arcade mastery, image discovery and tactical choices. The user then narrowed the visual exploration to four listed families:

1. **Ukrainian FPV military game in the Russian–Ukrainian war — MAIN.**
2. Ukrainian style, ethnicity, history and culture.
3. 1980s–1990s atmosphere and nostalgia.
4. Coupa business spend management and AI platform.

The wider space/ocean/fantasy ideas from round 02 remain archived exploration. The earlier suggestion to make tactical play secondary is superseded by the user's interest in all three motivations. Exact modes, difficulty and upgrade systems remain proposals.

## Current recommendation

One shared territory-reveal game with four complete visual/content families. Develop the main identity around **Daybreak Front**, then use **Night Signal** and **Steel Horizon** as chapter looks or selectable treatments. Keep the drone and enemy-role silhouettes stable across them. A player should still recognize the game when changing from the military family to culture, retro or Coupa.

Three motivations work at different moments: plan a route/module before a board, execute precise cuts during play, and discover artwork or animated details after a capture. The score challenge, collection and temporary build all grow from the same successful action. Do not put upgrade-reading tasks over live danger.

## 1. FPV FRONT — main family

| Treatment | Look | Best use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **W1 Daybreak Front — lead recommendation** | Golden fields, olive, graphite, chalk highlights and blue/yellow player; illustrated invading military presence in rural scenes | Initial identity; warm visual contrast and clear setting |
| **W2 Night Signal** | Ink-blue urban/industrial scenes, amber lights, restrained cyan and red signal accents | Atmospheric night chapters; strong soundtrack identity |
| **W3 Steel Horizon** | Copper, steel blue and weathered concrete; factories, rail yards and chunky military silhouettes | Industrial chapters and bold 1990s action-game personality |

The central character is a compact recognizable Ukrainian FPV quadcopter. It draws a vulnerable bright signal path through the unknown region. Closing a valid cut exposes part of a fictional illustrated scene containing invading Russian military forces. This keeps the user's requested image subject central. An optional recovery image can reward completion, but does not replace the military scene during play.

Proposed enemy roles are readable arcade abstractions: an open-field pursuer, a perimeter patrol, a stationary interference emitter, and a slow telegraphed hazard sweep. Map these roles onto hostile drone/vehicle/emitter silhouettes. Use an obvious shared mark for any moving enemy that prevents a region from filling. A captured emitter has a separate after-capture effect; it is not a secret fill blocker.

Maintain a strict distinction between lower-contrast military vehicles painted into reveal art and bright animated hazards that can hit the drone. A quiet inner border and large clear silhouettes matter more than detailed camouflage, tiny labels or many particles. The scene composition and behavior are fictional arcade design.

### First mission sequence to visualize next

1. Begin at a safe edge with one predictable moving threat and a partly concealed rural military illustration.
2. A small first cut exposes an invading vehicle and a strong scenic detail. Immediate sound and fill confirmation teach closure.
3. A larger optional cut can capture a marked emitter, disabling its visible arcade hazard after the region is filled.
4. Completion reveals the entire collectible scene and shows optional speed/survival/bold-cut medals.
5. A paused intermission offers one simple module choice before the next board: better safe-edge movement, a short enemy-path preview, or one visible trail protection.

This is a storyboarding proposal. Exact quota, lives, speed, fill policy and module effects are undecided. Main gameplay remains territory cutting; generated crosshair or weapon-like UI icons do not commit us to adding a separate shooting system.

Sound direction: original tracker drums and FM bass, bandura-like plucked accents, a compact hopeful melody; a synthetic signal pulse builds while the trail is exposed and resolves when the cut closes. Day/night/industrial chapters can use different arrangements of the same theme.

The contemporary arcade game [Death From Above](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2300160/Death_From_Above/?l=english) provides a published Ukrainian-drone thematic precedent. It is a different game and camera/control model; it does not supply our territory mechanics. [Volfied](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/arcade-archives-volfied-switch/) remains the closer genre precedent for a flying protagonist reclaiming territory.

## 2. UKRAINE ATLAS — culture, ethnicity and history

Three coherent collections can share a golden bird or stylus avatar:

- **Living Ornament:** separate Petrykivka, embroidery, pysanka, Kosiv and Crimean Tatar Örnek chapters. A completed trail might become stitching, a wax contour or a ceramic line according to the selected tradition. Ink blots, cracks and thorn shapes provide abstract hazards.
- **Chronicles of Ukraine:** period-specific illustrations, museum objects and historical scenes presented as an illustrated book. Each pack specifies an era and references, rather than merging centuries in one supposedly accurate image.
- **Modern Ukraine:** cities, architecture, design, music, science, industry and named artistic traditions. Culture can include present-day creative life as well as heritage.

Rewards: complete artwork, small ambient animation, a regional/era stamp, an optional concise caption, and an original music arrangement. History text belongs in the gallery, not on top of active play. [National Museum digital collections](https://collection.nmiu.org/), [Ivan Honchar collections](https://honchar.org.ua/collections/search), [UNESCO Petrykivka](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/petrykivka-decorative-painting-as-a-phenomenon-of-the-ukrainian-ornamental-folk-art-00893).

## 3. 1994 FOREVER — 80s–90s atmosphere

- **Arcade 1987:** black/cyan/magenta arcade field, geometric cursor, short FM jingles, original arcade-poster and cabinet-art rewards.
- **Amiga Summer — lead recommendation:** rich illustrated computer rooms, summer evenings, game shops, floppy disks and cassette shelves; warm pixel gradients, tactile UI and original tracker music.
- **Neon 1994:** city nights, chrome, music-video-like pixel scenes, cassette-cover collection and restrained synthwave/breakbeat arrangements.

These are art-direction names, not claims to emulate a particular machine exactly. A fictional cartridge shelf can organize level packs; a cassette deck can organize unlocked music. The avatar can be a tiny toy spacecraft or geometric signal cursor, with pixel glitches and perimeter sparks as threats. [UFO 50](https://50games.fun/) and [Narita Boy](https://www.team17.com/news/narita-boy-out-now-enter-the-digital-kingdom) supply useful precedents for coherent invented nostalgia.

## 4. NAVI NETWORK — Coupa BSM and AI

The main metaphor is a living business network: cuts recover useful connections and fictional savings; suppliers become reachable, matched documents align, workshops open and delivery routes resume. This supports the user's money-saving idea while also representing the broader platform.

- **Savings/Flow City:** animated client districts, mint routes, cobalt buildings and warm windows.
- **Navi Network:** connected buyers, suppliers, finance and helpful agents, with safe intermission choices inspired by forecasting and scenario exploration.
- **Value Factory / Paper Circuit:** playful request-to-outcome machinery, origami documents, stamps, matching stations and clear bottleneck puzzles.

Enemy proposals: duplicated records, a boundary-following bottleneck, a stationary exception emitter and a moving disruption cloud. Optional objectives can connect a supplier/request pair or recover order/receipt/invoice landmarks. Customers, suppliers and employees populate the world the player helps.

Module proposals: short enemy-path preview, a recharge for capturing matched tokens, faster movement on a recovered safe route, or better information about the next expedition board. These are authored arcade abilities; they do not require live AI calls to play and do not emulate actual Coupa workflows. In-game money or value points can represent the fictional reward economy.

Official documentation defines [Navi and Agent Studio](https://docs.coupa.com/en/coupa-glossary/overview/c), and Coupa's [Compose announcement](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/coupa-launches-coupa-compose-and-catalyst-to-accelerate-agentic-ai-value-and-delivery-at-inspire-2026-302769893.html) describes agent orchestration across procurement, finance and supply chain. The generated C token is an original placeholder; official game-character artwork and exact brand tokens remain to source.

## Devices and screen sizes

Recommend one complete provisional 4:3 arena whose game geometry stays unchanged when the window changes. Adapt the HUD, controls and surrounding artwork. If we later want a tall or panoramic arena, give it its own level/challenge identity; different geometry changes route risk and score comparability.

| Context / representative fixture | Layout recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Small portrait phone, 320px usable width | Compact status → complete board → one-line objective → large thumb-control deck |
| Larger portrait phone, 390–430px CSS width | Same hierarchy with larger controls and breathing room; tactical choices become a separate intermission |
| Landscape phone, about 667×375 or 844×390 CSS pixels | Board between a left movement rail and right action rail; safe areas respected |
| Tablet, portrait/landscape and resized windows | Large board; artwork/collection context can use a second pane during intermissions |
| Laptop/browser, about 1280×720 or 1440×900 | Complete arena, quiet mission rail, keyboard focus and remappable actions; no forced touch UI |
| PC handheld, 1280×800 | Complete arena, physical D-pad/stick, readable cards and correct controller hints |
| TV | Large distant-view HUD, obvious focus, all menus/controller actions available; finished picture can fill the screen in gallery mode |
| Ultrawide | Keep the arena intact; extend ambient scenery around it without exposing additional hazards |

Fixture sizes are design test cases, not hardware specifications or measurements of the generated images. All three motivations and all supported modes remain available across devices.

Each family changes the shell: field controller, embroidered pocket atlas, cartridge handheld, or Navi operations handset. Framing should be optional and should never consume the space needed for legible play.

Touch recommendation: test a four-way thumb pad outside the board first, then compare relative-swipe steering. D-pad/keyboard cardinal movement is the natural precision baseline. The exact draw/stop/ability model still needs visual explanation and testing. Follow [Apple game-control guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls) for comfortable frequent controls, and [Valve compatibility guidance](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/steamhardware/compat?l=english) for controller access and readable handheld UI.

## Shared content framework implications

Keep simulation, content packs, presentation, authoring and platform services separate. A complete family supplies its own image collections, player/enemy art, frame/UI, sounds, music, gallery metaphor, names and marketing compositions. Existing behavior roles remain shared; a ruleset can combine supported objective/module variations.

All families should support still reveal art, optional ambient layers, collectible metadata and alternate image states. Versioned packs allow new material without editing the main game. A new behavioral primitive still requires a bounded extension. Phaser/TypeScript remains provisional; this round introduces no new engine decision or platform-performance claim.

## Next discussion and proof

The immediate next design work is a larger main-theme mission storyboard with correctly drawn closure geometry, a measured portrait/landscape layout and a few enemy silhouettes at actual proposed size. Then compare the exact same board under the other three families. A later playable proof should test precise movement, understandable fill rules, fair danger, voluntary replay, real-device frame timing and theme replacement before the full asset set is produced.
