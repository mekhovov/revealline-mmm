# Round 14 — fulfillment audit against the original game request

Audited 12 September 2026 against frozen **v0.3.0**, source `7cd2046405a7d4daf6663c01c1478101bd0a632a`, and its [verified release](verification/round-13/release-v0.3.0.md). This is a requirements/content audit, with no game-code changes. The concurrent Round 14 progress-transfer UX work is outside this snapshot.

**The game is a working, extensible arcade release, but the user's complete content and play-experience goal is still partially fulfilled.** Native permissions, signing and hosting are not the only remaining work. The strongest gaps are authored picture rewards, a sustained challenge/mastery curve, animated enemy/content breadth, and complete controller navigation. Hundreds of correctness checks cannot close those gaps.

The original request asks for a beautiful, highly replayable Xonix game with XPOSED Reloaded as its main reference; Ukrainian FPV, Ukrainian culture, 80s–90s and Coupa themes; interchangeable assets and abilities; rich maps/bosses; rewarding picture reveals; saves, scores, galleries, expansions, music, multiple controls, tools and AI workflows. The [Round 08 gameplay plan](round-08-gameplay-plan.md) and [Round 10 implementation plan](round-10-implementation-plan.md) intentionally scoped early playable slices. Their smaller milestones do **not** replace the later instruction to finish the larger game.

Here, **implemented** means an actual current path exists; **partial** means a useful implementation leaves material requested scope unfinished; **not implemented** means the relevant behavior/content does not exist in the shipped runtime. These labels are not enjoyment or commercial-readiness certificates. Paths below point to maintained source; the frozen site/archive establishes the audited bytes.

## Concrete gaps that affect play

### 1. Collectible pictures need authored subjects, not only seeded rearrangements

All three shipped packs—Fieldcraft, Living Threads and Night Shift—have `visualOverrides: {}` and `levelVisuals: []`. No base level supplies artwork. `game/ui/scene-art.mjs` derives a seed from level/theme/revision, then varies a small set of village/landscape or city/grid constructions. The pictures differ deterministically; they are **not twenty-two separately composed, production-quality rewards**. Dawn and heritage share the same landscape branch, with heritage borders; arcade and network share city/grid construction.

The existing system already supports the needed next step: `packs.mjs` validates/merges a map's own `levelVisuals`, and the gallery uses the same binding. This is a content gap, not a reason to replace the engine. Cultural pictures need reviewed, identifiable subjects and captions; retro pictures need a broader range than neon city scenes; Spend Network needs authored business discoveries rather than renamed generic relays.

I inspected the actual [frozen completed picture](verification/round-13/screenshots/frozen-offline-picture.png) and the earlier [FPV art direction](concepts/round-06-fpv-object-skins.png). The shipped reward is a spare geometric village scene. The concept promises textured scenery, strong foreground silhouettes and military context. These are visibly different levels of detail. The concept is evidence of the requested direction, not evidence that its assets or interactive scenery shipped. The user's preference for both compact and detailed presentation can be retained while improving the **reward image**.

### 2. Map count overstates demonstrated encounter breadth

The [252-case balance survey](round-11-balance.md) found legal, no-life-loss clears of the original eighteen maps for every class and both turning modes **without using an ability, collecting supply, switching class or entering a signal zone**. Those clears took 5.10–16.37 simulated seconds, all earned gold, and used eleven distinct movement sequences. The first six expansion maps reused corresponding baseline routes. This proves accessible fallback routes; it does not establish a mature difficulty curve.

Fieldcraft is a material improvement: its four maps have [recorded role evidence and omission comparisons](fieldcraft-challenges.md), including entering interference, supply/emitter suppression, impact recovery and a net preventing cable contact. Its ordinary fallback routes remain intentional. However, four specialty encounters do not finish the original request for sustained campaign variety. Three of the four specialties make an equipment route faster; the impact route deliberately trades speed for recovery. These tradeoffs should become discoverable optional mastery goals, not hidden test-fixture facts.

The daily generator (`game/generator.mjs`) always uses two wall rectangles, two bouncers, one fixed required objective and a home supply. `challenges.mjs` makes calm/daily/expert variants by changing speed, lives and coverage. Dates give reproducible layouts within that template; they do not yield new encounter families, bosses or a roguelike campaign. Endless mode, boss rush and branching chapters are currently **proposals**, not implemented challenge modes or separately promised named features.

### 3. There is a lane boss, not a finished boss roster

The content inventory has four maps containing `lane-boss`: The Crossing, Last Light, Relay Storm and Garden of Memory. `core/index.mjs:updateBosses` periodically selects the player's row/column, warns, activates and returns to idle. The behavior is a useful legible hazard. It has no health/stage progression, capture-dependent behavior transition, dedicated counter-objective or distinct boss-family logic. A map named Afterglow Sentinel contains two bouncers and no boss.

The user explicitly asked for varied bosses. An entirely new simulation system is not necessary for the first improvement, but calling palette/axis/timer variants a complete boss roster would overstate the result. A bounded two-stage encounter with a clearly taught response is a better next proof than many renamed copies.

### 4. Player motion is ahead of enemy and world animation

The player uses the shared attachment rig and responds to motion. `BoardPainter.drawActor` immediately draws an imported enemy image as one static square and returns; there is no imported sprite-sheet/animation-state contract. Several procedural enemy glyphs also do not orient to their travel. Thus the user's request that major actors be beautifully animated and react to direction/speed is fulfilled for the player much more than for other actors.

All bouncers on a map share the `enemy` visual role, and map art cannot independently reskin two bouncers through entity-level bindings. Signal emitters, hangars, trails and ability effects are hand-drawn in the renderer outside the eight imported-image roles. The picture finale's three FPV equipment silhouettes are presentation objects; they do not correspond to the enemies or places affected during that run. There are real gameplay interactions, but the richer requested link between map subjects, actors and end-of-level payoff remains incomplete.

### 5. Long-term goals and equipment learning need another pass

`progress.mjs` has five achievements: first clear, clean clear, four clears, gold time and campaign completion. `unlockedBodies` grants the additional appearance groups after one and four unique clears. All seven classes are available independently of these cosmetics. This supports early rewards without a power grind, but the collectible path is largely exhausted before the twelve-map base campaign ends.

There are no role-use mastery achievements, chapter collections, optional per-map equipment objectives or authored late-campaign appearance rewards. Context-specific collection rules explored in the earlier motion lab are not fully carried into the runtime unlock model. The current How to Play text, mission briefs and first-cut demonstration teach core rules, but equipment learning mostly relies on text or leaving for read-only Replay Theater. A guided playable signal/supply/hangar lesson would bridge that gap.

### 6. Music genres exist; musical development needs listening and composition

`ui/music.mjs` has five default descriptors and a deterministic four-bar harmony/riff system. Three packs add descriptors selecting genre, tempo, tonic and scale. Tension changes orchestration, and settings offer short previews. These are real audio features, not silence or placeholder filenames.

The pack contract cannot carry authored notes, samples, stems, cue regions or custom SFX. Rock/metal use synthesized guitar-like voices rather than recorded performances. Existing tests use Web Audio doubles; they establish bounds and scheduling, not whether twenty minutes of play sounds good. There is no documented listener comparison, memorable chapter melody pass or fatigue/mix review. Full soundtrack production is a remaining content task; purchasing/licensing songs is an optional approach, not an original requirement.

### 7. Controller-only play stops at the interface boundary

`gamepadCommand` maps movement, boost, ability, pickup and pause. It does not map hangar selection or menu focus/confirm/back. Inactive/dialog states intentionally stop gameplay input; no separate controller UI router takes over. Thus a player can pilot with a controller but cannot be promised an uninterrupted controller-only session through starting, selecting a class, previewing a picture, retrying and switching at a hangar. This is a code gap, independently of the missing physical-controller test.

Solo keyboard remapping and tap/hold touch are substantial completed improvements. Remaining usability evidence should include a newcomer clearing several maps, understanding a failed cut, choosing an ability for a reason, and navigating all core screens without a mouse. Desktop mouse controls still have some 34×28 targets in the recorded layout, while phone/coarse fixtures use at least 44×44; the latter does not establish thumb reach, visibility under fingers or comfort during sustained play.

## Reference comparison and proposed extras

The [official XPOSED Reloaded listing](https://store.playstation.com/en-sa/concept/10002881), rechecked for this audit, emphasizes exposing pictures, distinctive backgrounds and progressively unlocked packs. That supports prioritizing an attractive collectible picture library and purposeful progression. It is a publisher description, not retention research. Earlier local frame studies already support gallery previews, preserved territory after failure and a picture-first result sequence; those are stronger foundations than inventing exact source physics.

The [Round 08 extension table](round-08-gameplay-plan.md) additionally proposed contour-following patrols, claimed-space enemies, erosion, player-only lethal/slow terrain and four contact pickup effects. Current interference partially addresses slowing, and stun/slow fields provide related abilities, but these are not equivalent to that entire table. Contour patrols, erosion, claimed-space enemies, life pickups and general timed contact pickups are **not implemented**. These are concrete prior design proposals informed by Reloaded observations; the original user requested broad reference fidelity, not necessarily every one of our proposed algorithms. Choose them for their gameplay value and define fairness before adding them.

Microsoft's [input accessibility guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/107), also rechecked, explicitly includes interface navigation and remapping throughout the game. This supports treating controller menus and updated prompts as part of input completeness. It does not certify this game's accessibility. A published pixel-art game such as [Celeste](https://www.celestegame.com/) can supply presentation inspiration, but its existence is not evidence that copying its mechanics would improve territory capture. The audit recommends improving our own content loop first.

## Evidence strengths and weaknesses

- **Strong correctness evidence:** 555 passing integrated tests in Round 13, deterministic replay verification, ordinary proofs for 24 campaign and 20 expansion configurations, a 308 map/class/turn fallback coverage baseline, Fieldcraft's 70 specialized proof/comparison records, and exact frozen build/archive checks. These are overlapping evidence sets, not 308 different levels or hundreds of human sessions.
- **Actual browser evidence:** saved collection/pack transfer, pause/resume, earned picture, both steering modes, offline reopening, workshop/couch availability and a complete verified Replay Theater run. The final release record includes a roughly three-second First Signal clear. That confirms a short legal success, not a balanced campaign or mastery ceiling.
- **Visual evidence is sampled:** the completed-picture screenshot was actually inspected. Concept sheets, scripted drawing-command tests and six CSS viewports do not prove finished art coverage, every themed map's readability or realistic phone performance.
- **Human-experience evidence is missing:** no documented representative-newcomer sessions, multi-level voluntary replay observations, perceived difficulty curve, soundtrack listening panel or sustained low-end-device frame/memory/thermal session. The game should not be labeled “perfect,” proven addictive or finished in those respects.
- **Authoring evidence has explicit boundaries:** thirteen skills/124 prompts and planned-asset records are useful working tools. They do not prove all requested assets were generated, art-directed, animated, installed and reviewed. Telegram's current intake has zero member originals.

## Next three highest-impact bounded steps

### 1. Ship one authored six-picture chapter through the existing pack system

Create a reviewed FPV Front chapter with six clearly different original rewards, then one fully applied example each for Ukraine Atlas, 1994 Forever and Spend Network. Give each image a subject/title/caption and a purpose in the chapter, rather than changing only palette or placement. Use `levelVisuals`; preserve original/derived art and provenance. Keep hostile military equipment and cultural/business subjects legible without importing real operational data. Retain compact/hybrid playfield options independently of the detailed rewards.

Acceptance: nine real assets are installed and visible in the game/gallery; each has a distinct readable composition at phone and desktop sizes; one image can be replaced and exported/reimported without code changes; original bytes survive backup; reveal/skip/reduced-effects leave each unobstructed. Add one restrained picture-specific visual payoff only after its anchor is clearly tied to that artwork. This closes the largest gap in the user's central reward loop without rebuilding the engine.

### 2. Add a short mastery chapter with a genuine boss progression

Use Fieldcraft's existing role interactions to author a four-map sequence: a playable equipment lesson, an optional equipment shortcut, a combined pressure map and one boss with two explicitly distinct stages. Preserve accessible ordinary clears. Add two or three versioned optional mastery conditions tied to actual events, such as emitter suppression plus a clean close or two separated supply-assisted captures; do not award them from the renderer. Reward a late chapter clear/mastery with a deliberate cosmetic choice. Calibrate medals from observed attempts as well as optimal proofs.

Acceptance: legal proof routes in both steering modes; action-omission comparisons demonstrate the selected abilities' value; boss stage transitions are visible and fair; retry explains why the attempt failed; several recorded human attempts establish what the lesson teaches and where time thresholds frustrate. This is a bounded extension toward the requested bosses/challenges, not a claim to finish an exhaustive military simulation.

### 3. Complete one whole-session control and feel pass

Add gamepad interface routing for play/continue, campaign/class selection, hangar, pause, results and gallery, plus remappable gameplay buttons and dead-zone choice. Preserve native keyboard/touch operation and show correct prompts. Run the new art/mastery chapter with keyboard-only, controller-only and touch controls, listening to all five music styles through actual audio output. Record rough edges and make a bounded soundtrack/feedback revision: one distinct chapter arrangement and differentiated capture/failure/boss/victory cues where the listening evidence calls for them.

Acceptance: a player can enter, play, fail, retry, change craft, win, view the picture and continue using one supported device without reaching for another; cancellation/disconnect never leaves motion stuck. Record comprehension, first/third-attempt timings and voluntary replay choice, plus sustained frame pacing on an agreed modest device. Native permission/signing/hosting work can continue separately; it must not consume the entire next iteration while these player-facing gaps remain.

These steps are recommendations for the next bounded iterations. They preserve the full original ambition and identify what they actually close; they do not redefine a smaller checklist as the complete game.
